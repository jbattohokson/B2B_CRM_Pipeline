Scope note: this is Olist's real seller-acquisition funnel. It records every marketing-qualified lead (MQL) and every lead that signed, but no deal amounts, pipeline stages or quotas. It can measure conversion and speed. It can't measure pipeline dollars, and because SDRs and closers are only recorded on won deals, it can't give a conversion rate per rep.

Executive Summary

Olist is a Brazilian marketplace that signs up merchants to sell through it. I modeled its published B2B funnel, 8,000 MQLs from June 2017 to May 2018 and the 842 that signed, into a dbt star schema on DuckDB, then built a Streamlit dashboard, a lead mix simulator and a local AI analyst on top.

Overall, 10.5% of MQLs signed, and the median deal closed 14 days after first contact. That headline hides a ramp: 2017 leads converted at 3.1% and waited a median of 216 days, while 2018 leads converted at 13.0% in 12.5 days. The sales team only started closing in December 2017.

Paid search (12.3%) and organic search (11.8%) convert above average and supply 48% of all MQLs. Social supplies 17% of MQLs but converts at 5.6%, and email at 3.0%. Their 95% intervals sit entirely below search's, so the gap isn't noise. In the simulator, moving 10 points of monthly volume from social to paid search adds about 10 deals a month at 2018 rates.

Interpretation

Social generates 16.9% of leads but only 8.9% of deals. Organic search does the opposite: 28.7% of leads and 32.2% of deals. Social leads also take twice as long to sign when they do (median 30 days vs 14.5). The intervals are computed with the Wilson method in a dbt macro, so a small source can't look like a winner by luck.

Data-quality flag, not a channel

The best-converting group is leads with no recorded source (16.7%). That could mean sales-sourced leads were never tagged. It's shown, but I don't recommend acting on it until someone finds out where those leads come from.

Why this matters for every other number

No deal closed before 2017-12-05. Leads from mid-2017 sat for months until a sales team existed to call them, so they convert worse and close slower for reasons unrelated to their source. Any channel or rep comparison that mixes 2017 and 2018 is distorted. The dashboard has a "2018 leads" filter and the simulator uses 2018 rates only.

Censoring handled

The 90-day line only appears for cohorts where every lead was at least 90 days old at the as-of date (2018-11-14). The May 2018 cohort's 180-day rate is left blank for the same reason. Without these window flags, young cohorts look worse than they are.

03Two-thirds of won deals sign within a month

554 of 842 won deals (66%) signed within 30 days of first contact. The median is 14 days, but the mean is 48.5 because of the 2017 backlog. One deal is dated 2 days before its first contact. It's flagged and excluded from cycle metrics, and a dbt test enforces that.

What this suggests

Among 2018 leads, 551 of 781 wins (71%) came within 30 days, and 144 (18%) came after day 60. Most of the value is in fast follow-up. Whether late chasing is worth SDR time is a question to test, since 18% of wins is not nothing.

04Rep output is concentrated in one SDR and one closer

SDR 14 sourced 140 deals (16.6%) and Rep 08 closed 133 (15.8%), across 32 SDRs and 22 closers. IDs in the source data are anonymized hashes; the readable labels are assigned in dbt.

What this can and can't say

These are volume and speed, not performance. Reps are only recorded on won deals, so a rep with many wins might also have many losses. A real CRM with rep ownership on every lead would allow per-rep conversion.

05Home decor and health and beauty sellers sign most often

Home decor (105 deals, median 13 days) and health and beauty (93, median 12) lead. Audio and video electronics sellers are slower (64 deals, median 22 days). Only 45 of 842 deals have a declared monthly revenue, so seller size isn't analyzed.

Planning Tool

Lead mix simulator

Expected deals = planned MQLs from each source × that source's 2018 conversion rate. The low and high bounds use each source's 95% interval. With no levers moved at the historical volume, it reproduces the 842 actual deals exactly (a test checks this).

Read it as a planning band

The range assumes every source lands at its bound at once, so it's wide on purpose. It also assumes extra paid search spend buys leads that convert like today's, which gets less true as spend scales.

Local AI

A free AI analyst that has to show its SQL

The dashboard's copilot is a CrewAI agent running on a local Ollama model (llama3.2, 3B), so it has no API cost. It answers by writing SQL against the marts. Every query and result is shown next to the answer, and a fact checker flags any number in the prose that didn't come from a query result.

What the live test shows

A free 3B model gets simple questions right and fails in two different ways on the rest. When its SQL fails, the guardrails catch it: the answer is marked ungrounded and every invented number is listed. When its SQL works but it misreads a column, nothing catches it, because every number it quotes is real. That second failure is why the app always shows the query and result table next to the prose. The unedited log is in docs/live_eval_llama3.2_2026-10-20.txt.

Next Steps

What I'd recommend, and what I'd test first

Channel mix

Shift budget from social toward paid search, in steps

Social converts at half the average. Move spend in 5-point steps and check that new paid search leads still convert near 14.8% (their 2018 rate).

Simulator: +10 points to paid search ≈ +10 deals / month

Data

Find out what "unknown" source leads are

1,159 leads with no source convert best of all. If they're referrals or sales-sourced, that channel deserves investment, but it's invisible today.

Audit lead capture for the 1,099 tagged "unknown" and 60 blank

Process

Test a 60-day follow-up cutoff

71% of 2018 wins closed inside 30 days, 18% after day 60. Split leads older than 60 days into "keep chasing" and "recycle" groups and compare.

Track: wins after day 60 per SDR hour spent

Measurement

Record the SDR on every lead, not just wins

Without it, rep coaching relies on volume. With it, per-rep conversion and speed become measurable.
